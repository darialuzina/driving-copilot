# CBR Assessment Criteria — How Errors Are Scored

> Source: CBR, Rijprocedure personenauto (B), versie juli 2026, §Toepassing.
> URL: https://www.cbr.nl/nl/voor-rijscholen/nl/rijprocedures/rijprocedure-b-1

The examiner does not score errors mechanically; the **total picture** of the exam
matters. Central is whether it is responsible to let the candidate participate in
traffic independently. If, at the final weighing, the examiner marks one or more
parts as onvoldoende, the exam result is onvoldoende (failed).

## Essential vs non-essential topics (Rijprocedure B, §Toepassing)

Each exam part has **essential** assessment topics (essentiële onderwerpen van
beoordeling) and non-essential ones. Both are weighed, but essential topics carry
more weight. Examples:

- **Beheersing van het voertuig** (vehicle mastery) is always essential (§1.4).
- **Aangepast en besluitvaardig gedrag** and **belangen andere weggebruikers** are
  essential (§2.1, §2.2) and must be **structural** to count as blameworthy.
- **Bediening koppeling, schakelmechanisme, gastoevoer en remmen** are **not
  essential** — *unless* the action has or can have a large influence on traffic
  safety, in which case it can be marked essential. (§Toepassing Hoofdstuk 1.)

## The AEX factors (§Toepassing Inleiding)

How much a not- or poorly-executed action weighs depends on the **AEX** factors:

- **A — de aard** (the nature): the concrete action or omission.
- **E — de ernst** (the severity): the degree to which execution deviates from the
  prescribed behaviour.
- **X — het aantal malen** (the count): how often the action occurs in relation to
  the part.

Road, weather and traffic circumstances are also weighed; deviating behaviour can
still be considered acceptable under certain circumstances.

## What makes you fail

- **Structurally deviant basic behaviour** (chapter 1: rijklaar maken and
  bediening/beheersing) means the candidate cannot pass, even if individual parts go
  well. (§Toepassing Hoofdstuk 1.)
- **Insufficient vehicle mastery** — total inability to operate the car
  rijtechnisch, or insufficient stuurvastheid — is essential. It must be **structural**
  (across multiple parts, or frequently within one part) to be blameworthy, **except
  at the bijzondere verrichtingen**, where it can lead to an onvoldoende on its own.
- **Insufficiently adapted or non-decisive behaviour** means an onvoldoende exam.
  (§Toepassing Hoofdstuk 2.)
- One or more parts marked onvoldoende at the final weighing → exam result
  onvoldoende.

## Can you fail for stalling?

Stalling (afslaan van de motor) relates to **bediening koppeling** (§1.2 /
§Toepassing Hoofdstuk 1). Bediening koppeling is **not essential** on its own, so a
single stall is not automatically a fail. However:

- If stalling has or could have a **large influence on safety** (e.g. stalling on a
  busy intersection, a roundabout, or while merging), it can be marked essential and
  weighed with the AEX factors.
- **Repeated** stalling, or stalling as part of **structural** poor basic behaviour /
  insufficient vehicle mastery, can lead to a fail.
- At the **bijzondere verrichtingen**, where vehicle mastery in a limited space is the
  point, poor clutch control can already lead to an onvoldoende for that part without
  needing the structural pattern required elsewhere.

So: a one-off stall in a calm spot is typically not a fail; repeated stalling or
stalling that creates danger can be.
